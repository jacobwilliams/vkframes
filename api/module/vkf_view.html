Camera views: where the camera looks from, as OpenFrames' View.

A view follows a frame: the camera's trackball (eye, center, up, which the mouse moves) is kept in the view frame's coordinates, so the camera moves with the frame. The view frame is * RELATIVE_FRAME (the default): the viewed frame itself (a body-fixed view); * ABSOLUTE_FRAME: the root frame's axes, at the viewed frame's origin (an inertial view that follows the frame as it moves). A view between frames (set_view_between_frames) looks from one frame at another: the view frame is turned so that its +y axis (the trackball's initial viewing direction) points at the second frame, in one direct rotation (VIEW_DIRECT) or first in azimuth, then elevation (VIEW_AZEL, the default, which keeps z up).

The default camera (reset_view) looks along +y at the frame's bound from the distance at which it fills the field of view (or from default_view_distance, or from set_default_view_parameters). Views are not owned by windows: a view, and the frames it refers to, must stay alive while used.

The camera (OpenFrames' trackball get/setTransformation): a window's get_camera / set_camera for the view it shows (and smooth transitions, see vkf_window), or View%set_camera for a view's camera when it is next shown. camera_between interpolates between two cameras (each eye, center, up): the center along a straight line, the distance geometrically, the orientation by the shortest rotation.

public pure function camera_between(s, from, to) result(cam)

The camera a fraction s (0 to 1) of the way from camera from to camera to (columns: eye, center, up; in one frame's coordinates): the center moved along a straight line, the distance to it changed by the same factor at each step (so far-to-near flights don't rush the near part), and the orientation turned by the shortest rotation.
