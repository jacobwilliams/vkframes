A growing set of points drawn from a chain of GPU buffers ("chunks"), for the trajectory artists: a line strip, separate segments (pairs of points), markers (one per point) or a ribbon (a translucent surface through pairs of points: a quad between each pair and the next). Points are written in place, as (high, low) float pairs for double precision (see src/vsg/vkf_layer.cpp: EyeRelative), with a strip's distance along it (for dash patterns, see shaders/line.vert; measured from the trajectory's points), and only the chunks written to are uploaded again. A full chain gets new chunks, each as large as the chain so far or as what is needed at once, but at least min_chunk and at most max_chunk points, so nothing is ever copied or reallocated, and changing a point uploads at most max_chunk points again. In a strip, consecutive chunks share their end point so that the line is continuous; in a ribbon, their end pair.

Level of detail (strips): seen from far away, a dense trajectory is mostly segments shorter than a pixel. For each chunk, the chain keeps the bounding sphere of its points and, for strides 2, 4, ... 2^lod_levels, a bound on how far the points skipped can be from the segments drawn (every stride-th point and the last), and gives them to the renderer (vkf_line_set_lod), which picks the stride per view (src/vsg/vkf_layer.cpp: EyeRelative). The bounds are built level by level: a segment at stride 2s is within the larger of its two halves' bounds, plus its middle point's distance from it, of the points it skips (the halves' segments lie within that distance of it). They are updated only for the segments the points written change.

Writing points i1 .. i2 (indices along the whole chain):

call chain%reserve(i2)
do j = 1, chain%num_chunks()
    call chain%slice(j, i1, i2, lo, hi, p)      ! p: points lo .. hi of chunk j
    if (.not. associated(p)) cycle
    ... fill p(1:3, :, 1:hi-lo+1) (e.g. with split_points) ...
end do
call chain%written(i1, i2)                     ! measure (strips) and upload
call chain%draw(i2)
