Windows showing a scene of frames, with its simulation time, as OpenFrames' FrameManager and WindowProxy (for now with a single viewport, which holds the views as OpenFrames' RenderRectangle does; OpenFrames' grid of viewports comes later).

A FrameManager holds the root frame of a scene. A WindowProxy shows one: in a window of its own, or in a host's native window (e.g. a Qt QWindow's winId()), in which case the host forwards the window's events (mouse, scroll, key, resize, close). Each frame() advances the simulation clock, updates the frames (followed trajectories), copies their positions and attitudes to the renderer, places the camera for the current View and draws.

Several windows can show one scene (its render nodes are shared). The scene is updated once per simulation time: a window whose time is the one the scene was last updated for, by another window since this one's last frame, doesn't update it again (FrameManager%update). Windows whose clocks are synchronized (SimulationClock%synchronize) render the same time, their master's at its last frame, so they show the scene in the same state and update it once per round of frames, also while the time is paused (then each round still updates once, for data added meanwhile).

A window has a list of views (add_view, select_view, next_view, ...); each keeps its own camera (the trackball) while another one is shown. Without views, a default view of the scene's root frame is used.

Keyboard and mouse (OpenFrames' WindowProxy callbacks, setKeyPressCallback, setMouseMotionCallback, setButtonPressCallback, setButtonReleaseCallback): a program's WindowEventHandler, an extension overriding the procedures it wants (key_press, key_release, mouse_motion, button_press, button_release), is given the window's events (set_event_handler), also those a host forwards. They're passed on after the frame in which they arrived (from frame()), so a handler may change anything, the window included (its views, its time, closing it). Positions are in -1 to 1 across the window, y up, as OpenFrames'. The built-in keys, as OpenFrames': v the next view, V the previous one, the space bar resets the view (unless the handler's key_press says it handled the key, or set_default_keys(.false.)). The mouse moves the camera (the trackball); the keys don't.

Each frame, the handler's before_draw(window, t) is called once the scene is at the frame's time and the camera is placed (the trackball's moves included), just before drawing: what it changes (HUD text, labels, frames' positions) is in this frame, e.g. a callout kept next to a moving object (screen_position) without lagging a frame behind, whoever runs the frame loop (the program or a host GUI).

The camera (OpenFrames' trackball getTransformation / setTransformation): get_camera and set_camera (eye, center, up in the shown view's frame, or in its root frame with in_root). Smooth transitions (not in OpenFrames): set_camera with a duration flies there, and with set_view_transition(seconds) (default 0: a cut, as OpenFrames) changing the view (select_view, next_view, previous_view, the keys v and V; or with their own duration) and resetting it (the space bar) fly from the camera shown to the new one. A flight moves the center along a straight line, the distance geometrically and the orientation by the shortest rotation, eased in and out, in the root frame (both views' frames as they move), over the wall-clock time given (the video's while recording); a mouse press, stop_transition or another flight ends it.

Video recording (not in OpenFrames, which has only screenshots): start_recording(file, fps) ... stop_recording() records the frames drawn to a video file, encoded by ffmpeg (conda-forge's, in the pixi environment; or ffmpeg, or VKFRAMES_FFMPEG): the frames are piped to it as raw pixels (no image files; see vkf_viewer_record_start in src/vsg/vkf_layer.cpp). While recording, the window's time is the video's: each frame advances the simulation time by exactly scale/fps, and camera flights and animated shaders (pulsing markers, highlighted lines) by 1/fps s, so the video plays smoothly at its own speed however long a frame takes to draw (or to encode). Stopping goes back to the wall clock from where the video was. A watermark (e.g. a logo) can be drawn over the video by ffmpeg (watermark). The video's size is its first frame's (a window resized while recording is cropped or padded).

The mouse's sensitivity: set_zoom_speed (the zoom per wheel or trackpad step and per right drag; default 1, VSG's) and set_camera_throw (the camera keeps moving after a drag released while moving; default on).

Picking (not in OpenFrames): pick(x, y, hits) gives what is under a point of the window (x, y as the event handler's: -1 to 1, y up), nearest first, as drawn at the last frame: trajectory curves (the point and the time there), segments and markers (within a few pixels: tolerance, plus half a line's width or a marker's size), bodies (the point on the surface, with latitude and longitude) and frames' origins. Each frame of the scene tests its own contents (ReferenceFrame%pick, vkf_pick); nothing is done until pick is called. cursor_ray(x, y, origin, direction) gives the ray from the eye through a point of the window (root frame), e.g. to place something where the cursor points (vkf_pick's ray_plane, ray_sphere, ray_line).

Handles (vkf_handles: DragHandle, e.g. VectorHandle): while a scene shown in a window has handles, the window looks for one under the cursor at each mouse move (a pick of PICK_HANDLE only), hovers it (drawn brighter) and arms a grab: a left button press then grabs the handle instead of moving the camera, the moves drag it (DragHandle%drag, with the cursor's ray) and the release lets it go. The handler is told (handle_drag: DRAG_START, DRAG_MOVE, DRAG_END), not given those presses, moves and releases (button_press, mouse_motion, button_release). A handle is dragged from the frame after the event's, as other events are handled. The middle and right buttons move the camera over handles too.
