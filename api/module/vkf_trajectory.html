Trajectory data: times with positions, attitudes and optional data vectors, as in OpenFrames' Trajectory.

Data are appended: each time may get one position (with num_optionals optional vectors), one attitude and one color, so there may be fewer positions, attitudes or colors than times (data can arrive separately). Colors are optional (not in OpenFrames): a CurveArtist draws a trajectory with colors in them, blended along each segment between its points' colors (see CurveArtist%set_point_colors). No sorting is done; times are expected to be monotonic, increasing or decreasing. Indices are 1-based.

Differences from OpenFrames: * Storage grows by doubling, positions, attitudes and colors separately, so a trajectory without attitudes (colors) holds no attitude (color) memory. reserve sets it for a known size; compact frees what's unused once a trajectory is complete (one copy of each array; storage_bytes says what is held). * Instead of subscriber callbacks, a trajectory counts its changes (revision) and clears (generation): a reader such as an artist compares them with what it last saw and updates only what changed (e.g. uploads only the points appended since). * No locking: vkframes updates and renders on one thread.

Between points, a trajectory is linear in time: point_at(t) is the point at any time (the first or last point outside its times), and point_between(k, k2, t) and time_fraction(k, k2, t) the same between two points already found (followers, trace mode's tail, intermediate markers and time handles use them).

private subroutine compact(self)

Free the storage not used: each array is reallocated to the points it holds (one copy, an array at a time), and arrays holding none are deallocated. For a trajectory that is complete: growing doubles the storage, so up to about half of it can be unused. The data and what is drawn of them don't change; pointers from times, positions, ... must be fetched again. Points can still be added (storage grows again).
